Hey there, friend — welcome to DadCyberTips, your friendly neighborhood dad for all things internet.

I'm not going to pretend I'm some Silicon Valley genius or a hacker in a hoodie. I'm just a dad who got tired of watching people I care about — family, friends, coworkers, that one neighbor who still can't figure out his email — get tripped up by the same little tech problems over and over. A sketchy email here. A weak password there. A phone update nobody wants to deal with. Sound familiar?

Here's the thing about being a dad: you don't need a PhD to help someone. You just need patience, a little know-how, and the willingness to explain something twice (or three times) without making anyone feel dumb for asking. That's exactly what I want this space to be. No gatekeeping. No tech-bro superiority. No lectures about how you "should have known better."

So I started writing this stuff down the way I'd explain it at the dinner table: no jargon, no scare tactics, no assuming you already know this. Just straightforward, practical advice — the digital equivalent of "check your tire pressure" or "don't leave the porch light off."

My goal is simple: help everyone — grandparents, teenagers, small business owners, the works — feel a little more confident and a little safer online. Whether you're setting up two-factor authentication for the first time, wondering if that text from "your bank" is legit, or just trying to remember which password goes with which account (we've all been there), I've got you.

I'll cover the everyday stuff that actually matters: spotting scams before they cost you money, locking down your accounts without losing your mind, keeping your kids' devices safe, and cutting through the tech noise so you can actually enjoy using your devices instead of fearing them.

Think of me as the guy down the street who's happy to help you jump-start your car, except it's your Wi-Fi router and your inbox. No judgment, no condescension — just someone who wants to see you do well.

Grab a coffee, stick around, and let's tackle the little things — together. That's what dads are for.
