## Start With the Digital Front Door

One of the first things the toolkit addresses is something many families overlook: **their home network**.

Your router is essentially the front door to the connected devices inside your home.

The checklist walks parents through practical steps such as changing the router's default administrator password, using modern Wi-Fi encryption, creating a guest network for visitors and IoT devices, keeping router firmware updated, and disabling remote management when it isn't needed.

From there, you can work through the devices your family actually uses.

Phones. Tablets. Computers. Gaming devices.

Automatic updates, screen locks, backups, device-finding capabilities and knowing how to remotely wipe a lost phone are all part of the process.

## Privacy Isn't Just About Technology

This is one of the biggest ideas behind DadCyberTips.

**Technology can help protect your family, but conversations protect your family too.**

The toolkit encourages age-appropriate parental controls while emphasizing something particularly important for older kids: **open conversation can be more valuable than surveillance.**

That means talking with your kids about:

- What information should stay private
- Who they communicate with online
- What they share in games and social media
- Why location sharing matters
- What to do when something online makes them uncomfortable
- How to recognize suspicious messages

The objective isn't to make children afraid of the internet.

**It's to help them become capable of navigating it.**

## Teach Your Family to Slow Down

Scams and phishing attacks aren't just a technology problem.

They're a **human problem**.

The toolkit includes a simple family approach to recognizing common warning signs: urgency, strange links, requests for passwords or gift cards, and suspicious messages from supposed friends or contacts.

One of the simplest rules you can establish at home is:

> **If something online pressures you to act immediately, stop and verify it first.**

That one habit can prevent a lot of bad decisions.

## Make It a Family Habit

Security shouldn't become a once-a-year project.

The toolkit recommends a **15-minute monthly family digital checkup** where you install pending updates, review newly installed apps, check screen-time reports, confirm privacy settings and work through an account security check.

Fifteen minutes.

That's it.

Because the goal isn't perfect security.

**The goal is making your family's digital safety part of your family's normal routine.**
